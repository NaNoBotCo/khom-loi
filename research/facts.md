## 1. Construction

### 1.1 Legal size and fuel limits (the number to draw)
- Lantern body: volume not over 1 m³, diameter not over 90 cm, height not over 140 cm, natural materials. A body over 1 m³ needs a permit under the Air Navigation Act "because it counts as an aircraft" (เนื่องจากเข้าลักษณะอากาศยาน). [CONFIRMED] — ประกาศจังหวัดสมุทรสาคร, Royal Gazette เล่ม 133 ตอนพิเศษ 258 ง หน้า 40–41, 14 Nov 2016 (B.E. 2559). https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/2089971.pdf
- Fuel: "กระดาษชุบเทียนขี้ผึ้ง หรือพาราฟิน" (paper soaked in beeswax candle wax or paraffin), weight not over 55 g, burn time not over 8 minutes, hung from the body by 2 strands of fire-resistant string or soft No. 24 wire (≈0.5 mm diameter), each strand not over 30 cm. [CONFIRMED] — same gazette notice as above.
- The same notice says size and form follow Thai Community Product Standard มผช. 808/2552 (โคมลอย). [CONFIRMED that the notices cite it] — same gazette; also ประกาศจังหวัดภูเก็ต, Royal Gazette เล่ม 133 ตอนพิเศษ 241 ง หน้า 19, 19 Oct 2016. https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/2086360.pdf
- มผช. 808/2552 text itself: [UNCONFIRMED] — TISI community-standard PDF (tcps.tisi.go.th) did not load; the figures above are the gazette's restatement.
- Chiang Mai applies the same limits (90 cm / 140 cm / natural materials / 55 g fuel / 8 min, fuel tied with fire-proof cord, maker's name and warnings on the product). [SECONDARY] — MGR Online, 18 Oct 2023. https://mgronline.com/local/detail/9660000094022
- CAAT airspace notices repeat the limits (diameter ≤ 90 cm, height ≤ 140 cm, volume ≤ 1 m³, combustion ≤ 8 min). [CONFIRMED] — CAAT AIP SUP A 52/24 (Chiang Rai, VTCT), effective 14–16 Nov 2024. https://aip.caat.or.th/2024-11-14/html/eSUP/VT-eSUP-24-52-A-en-GB.html
- Fuel described as tissue paper and wax "less than one centimetre thick and ten centimetres long". [SECONDARY, single source] — Chiang Mai CityLife (CityNews), 9 Nov 2015. https://chiangmaicitylife.com/news/lantern-rules-and-regs

### 1.2 Paper and frame
- Body paper: "กระดาษว่าว" (kite paper) on a bamboo frame. [SECONDARY] — TrueID Travel, 22 Nov 2018. https://travel.trueid.net/detail/6gJreZkxbq7
- Thai Wikipedia says bamboo frame with กระดาษสา (mulberry paper). [SECONDARY, lead only] — th.wikipedia.org/wiki/โคมลอย (read 2026-10-03).
- So the paper is variously kite/tissue paper or sa paper; no source here measures which dominates in Chiang Mai today. [UNCONFIRMED]
- Chiang Mai University's sa-paper research unit (หน่วยวิจัยกระดาษสา, Faculty of Science) developed flame-retardant sa paper for export lanterns: self-extinguishing, no afterglow, char length under 5 cm; also tested replacements for the metal wire. [CONFIRMED] — NSTDA Annual Conference NAC2011 slides "จากกระดาษสาทนไฟ สู่โคมลอยทนไฟส่งออก", PDF created 30 Mar 2011. https://www.nstda.or.th/nac/2011/presentation/ppt_nac2011_CombLoy.pdf
- Same slides: San Pa Tong lantern community enterprise exported 200,000 lanterns/yr before 2010, 20,000 in 2010 (domestic 30,000/yr both periods), after EU rule problems. [CONFIRMED as stated in slides]
- Construction described by TU Delft: impregnated rice or mulberry paper envelope on a bamboo ring; burner = cloth saturated with paraffin wax, hung at the centre of the ring by two perpendicular strings; some have a fire-resistant sleeve under the pad. [CONFIRMED] — Schuurman & Gransden, "Sky Lantern Safety Flight Profile for Risk Assessment", AIAA Balloon Systems Conference, 5–9 Jun 2017, AIAA 2017-3289, doi:10.2514/6.2017-3289 (TU Delft accepted manuscript). https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_b7cf8f82-8735-4a86-acdd-2c99d78d77ee
- Old-style recipe from a reader's letter: 14 sheets of kite paper, 2 for the crown, 3 for each of the four sides, cloth scraps as fuel. [UNCONFIRMED — anecdote relayed in a Chinese-language blog repost] — The News Lens, 22 Nov 2018. https://www.thenewslens.com/article/108571

### 1.3 The fuel — the right word
- Official notices: "เชื้อเพลิง" / "ส่วนที่เป็นเชื้อเพลิง". [CONFIRMED] — Royal Gazette notices above.
- Lanna description of ว่าวไฟ: heat comes from a "ลูกไฟ" (fire-ball) tied to the central core; "in the past cast as a stick from ขี้ย้า; today usually toilet paper soaked in candle wax" (ในอดีตนั้นใช้ขี้ย้าหล่อเป็นแท่ง ปัจจุบันนิยมใช้กระดาษชำระชุบขี้ผึ้งเทียน). [SECONDARY] — Payap University Library, Lanna custom sheet "ประเพณียี่เป็ง" (compiled from TrueID, 1 Nov 2021). https://library.payap.ac.th/webin/NTIC/Lanna%20custom/word/pd00012.pdf
- Meaning of ขี้ย้า: [UNCONFIRMED] — no dictionary source found; possibly a resin, needs a Lanna dictionary check.
- ขี้ไต้: not found in any lantern source read. ไต้ is a resin torch; using it for lantern fuel is [UNCONFIRMED].
- ไส้ / ไส้โคม: common in shop talk but [UNCONFIRMED] — not found in an official or academic text here.
- Suggested labels: ลูกไฟ (Lanna, the burning ball), เชื้อเพลิง (official), กระดาษชุบขี้ผึ้ง/พาราฟิน (what it is).

### 1.4 Measured masses, volumes, burn times
- TU Delft, 38 lanterns of 5 commercial types (Netherlands market): envelope volumes 104.6–239.8 L; average total mass 53.55–113.78 g. Types: A pear 125.5 L / 53.55 g; B heart 104.6 L / 59.32 g; C cylinder 165.8 L / 87.85 g; D heart XL 239.8 L / 113.78 g; E pear XL 194.0 L / 90.32 g. [CONFIRMED] — Schuurman & Gransden 2017 (above), Table 2.
- Fuel cell ≈ 30% of lantern weight, depending on type. [CONFIRMED] — same paper.
- Burn times measured 100–330 s; modelled flight times 200–600 s; smaller lanterns burned at least 100 s, type A averaged about 200 s. [CONFIRMED] — same paper.
- One type E lantern's crown caught fire at 105 s, dropping lift to zero with the fuel still lit. [CONFIRMED] — same paper.
- Lift forces measured were "on the order of decanewtons" with up-to-10 N rig range. [CONFIRMED] — same paper. (Note: decanewton phrasing is the paper's; the lanterns weigh ~0.5–1.1 N.)
- Chiang Mai University lab lanterns (tealight, 0.159 m³): "traditional" shape 28.70 g; cuboid 32.28 g; spherical 34.12 g; small ellipsoid 27.70 g. [CONFIRMED] — Wattanakasiwich, Kongkhumbod & Pussadee, "Heating up a lantern with a tealight candle", Revista Mexicana de Física E 19 010206 (Jan–Jun 2022), Dept. of Physics and Materials Science, Chiang Mai University. https://doi.org/10.31349/RevMexFisE.19.010206
- Typical size of a Yi Peng festival lantern (height, mouth diameter, empty mass): [UNCONFIRMED] — no Thai maker or measurement found; only the legal caps above.

### 1.5 Inner temperature and lift (published physics)
- Lift-off condition: (ρ_air − ρ_hot)·V > M, with ρ = P/(R_s·T), R_s = 287.058 J/(kg·K). Solved: T_hot > P·T / (P − (M/V)·R_s·T). [CONFIRMED] — Wattanakasiwich et al. 2022 (eq. 3–6). Their printed eq. 2 has a typo (F_B − g for F_B − mg).
- Chiang Mai lab conditions: P = 96.5 kPa, T = 300.8 K (27.65 °C). Required inner temperature: traditional 85.8 ± 7.7 °C; small ellipsoid 83.1 ± 7.5 °C; cuboid 94.4 ± 8.0 °C; spherical 99.3 ± 8.3 °C. [CONFIRMED] — same paper, Table II.
- Peak measured inner temperature by IR camera: traditional 95.0 °C; small ellipsoid 89.3 °C; cube 82.3 °C; spherical 71.6 °C. [CONFIRMED] — same paper, Table III.
- Heat-up times to float 2.5 m: traditional 5 min 23 s heating + 4.29 s rise; small ellipsoid 4:00 + 5.01 s. [CONFIRMED] — same paper, Table IV.
- Drag coefficients used: cylinder 0.82, cuboid 1.05, sphere 0.47 (from their ref. 5). [CONFIRMED] — same paper, Table I. TU Delft used c_D = 0.3 for ascent. [CONFIRMED] — Schuurman & Gransden 2017.
- TU Delft model: L = (ρ∞ − ρ_i)gV; standard-atmosphere P(h), T(h) with lapse 6.5×10⁻³ K/m; mass falls linearly from ignition to flame-out; after flame-out free fall to terminal velocity. [CONFIRMED] — same paper, eq. 1–11.
- [COMPUTED] Lift per m³ at Chiang Mai pressure 96.5 kPa: ambient 25 °C, inner 95 °C → Δρ = 0.214 kg/m³ → 214 g of lift per m³. Ambient 20 °C, inner 95 °C → 234 g/m³. At the legal maximum of 1 m³ that is roughly 0.2 kg of lift. At CMU's lab ambient (27.65 °C) and 85.8 °C, a 0.159 m³ lantern lifts 28.8 g — matching the 28.70 g lantern mass in their table.
- Late-November night air temperature in Chiang Mai: not researched here. [UNCONFIRMED]

### 1.6 Flight height, time, drift
- Dutch NVWA 2009 field flights (10 untracked launches, estimated by KNMI staff): durations 120–300 s; heights 50–300 m; distances 633–1,900 m. [SECONDARY via TU Delft Table 1] — NVWA/VWA "Verkenning van de veiligheid van wensballonnen", ZW 09231d, 2009, as tabulated in Schuurman & Gransden 2017.
- TU Delft summary page: cruising above 300 m "is common", average 500 m; all balloons land within ten minutes. [CONFIRMED as stated] — TU Delft Aerospace Engineering, "Fiery romance: a risk model for sky lanterns" (undated spotlight, refers to 2017 work). https://www.tudelft.nl/en/ae/research/spotlight/fiery-romance-a-risk-model-for-sky-lanterns/
- Manufacturer claims up to 1 mile (1,600 m). [CONFIRMED as a claim reported] — Schuurman & Gransden 2017.
- A pilot reported lanterns at 13,000 ft on approach to Chiang Mai, 11 Nov 2019 ("ตั้งแต่ 13,000 ft radial 172 40 dme เจออยู่ 3 อัน"). [SECONDARY, single pilot's Facebook post] — MGR Online, 12 Nov 2019. https://mgronline.com/local/detail/9620000108352 ; Thai PBS, 12 Nov 2019. https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/286022
- "Lanterns reach 33,000 ft": appeared in a search snippet only. [UNCONFIRMED]
- No published drift-distance study for Chiang Mai was found. [UNCONFIRMED]

## 2. Vocabulary (Thai / Lanna)

- ยี่เป็ง (yii-peng): ยี่ = month two (เดือนยี่), เป็ง = full moon (central Thai เพ็ญ). Lanna month 2 falls with central Thai month 12, so ยี่เป็ง = วันเพ็ญเดือน 12. [CONFIRMED] — ศิลปวัฒนธรรม (Silpa-mag), วรชาติ มีชูบท, from Silpa Watthanatham Nov 2016; online 11 Nov 2019. https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_4146
- Why the shift: the 1888 royal notice "ประกาศให้ใช้วันอย่างใหม่" says the northern cities had skipped intercalary months (อธิกมาศ) "for some two years". The 2019 Silpa version says the north is shifted 2 months; the 2023 re-post of the same text says 1 month. [CONFIRMED that the two versions disagree; the 2-month figure fits month 2 ↔ month 12] — Silpa-mag 4146 (11 Nov 2019) vs https://www.silpa-mag.com/culture/article_41254 (27 Nov 2023), citing Royal Gazette vol. 5 p. 451–456 (1888).
- Chiang Mai celebrates on two nights: วันเพ็ญ and วันแรม 1 ค่ำ เดือนยี่เหนือ. [CONFIRMED] — Silpa-mag 4146.
- Lanna name for lanterns that fly is ว่าว (waaw): "a paper plaything let float with the wind, like a balloon". Two kinds at Yi Peng: [SECONDARY] — Payap University Library sheet (above).
  - ว่าวฮม (waaw hom) = ว่าวลม / ว่าวควัน: filled with hot smoke (ฮมควัน), released by day, often with a string of firecrackers on its tail; central Thai โคมลอย.
  - ว่าวไฟ (waaw fai): same principle, less paper, carries a burning ลูกไฟ, released at night; central Thai โคมไฟ.
- Today's officialese: โคมลอย (sky lantern, night), โคมไฟ, โคมควัน (smoke lantern, daytime), equated with ว่าวฮม by AOT: "ปล่อยโคมควัน (ว่าวฮม)". [CONFIRMED] — AOT press release 89/2567, 11 Nov 2024. https://motwebservice.mot.go.th/motpublichearing/uploads/19-11-24_3439_89_2567%20AOT%20ย้ำเตือนอันตรายจากการปล่อยโคม.pdf
- Note the naming flip: in Payap's Lanna sheet, ว่าวฮม ↔ central "โคมลอย" and ว่าวไฟ ↔ central "โคมไฟ"; in present-day usage โคมลอย means the night fire lantern. Worth one line on the page. [CONFIRMED as two sources differing]
- ว่าวลม: given as the gloss of ว่าวฮม. [SECONDARY] — Payap sheet.
- โคมแขวน (khom khwaen, hanging lantern), โคมถือ (hand lantern), โคมผัด (khom phat, rotating lantern turned by the flame's heat), โคมลอย/ว่าวไฟ, ว่าวควัน. [SECONDARY, lead] — th.wikipedia.org/wiki/ยี่เป็ง (read 2026-10-03).
- Government PR Dept lists ผางประทีป (clay oil/wax lamps), โคมรังมดส้ม, โคมกระบอก, โคมผัด for Yi Peng. [CONFIRMED] — กรมประชาสัมพันธ์ (prd.go.th), 13 Jun 2026. https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/31/iid/510215
- ปล่อยโคม (to release a lantern): used throughout official notices (จุดและปล่อย). [CONFIRMED] — Royal Gazette notices, AOT release.
- Readings (RTGS): khom loi, khom fai, khom khwan (โคมควัน), khom khwaen (โคมแขวน), wao hom (ว่าวฮม), wao fai (ว่าวไฟ), yi peng. Tone/IPA not researched. [UNCONFIRMED for tones]

### 2.1 What the release means
- Offering to พระเกศแก้วจุฬามณี (the Culamani stupa in Tavatimsa heaven, ดาวดึงส์): PR Dept says Yi Peng honours พระเกศแก้วจุฬามณี and the lanterns carry away sorrow and misfortune. [CONFIRMED] — prd.go.th, 13 Jun 2026 (above).
- Birth-year relic link: in Lanna belief พระธาตุจุฬามณี in Tavatimsa is the relic for people born in the Year of the Dog (ปีจอ); Wat Ketkaram, Chiang Mai is the earthly stand-in. [SECONDARY] — KTC travel article, 16 Jan 2025. https://www.ktc.co.th/article/travel-stories/thailand/th/phra-that-of-12-star
- That ว่าวฮม were sent up with names tied on as an offering to that relic: [UNCONFIRMED — the blog author says she could not verify it] — The News Lens, 22 Nov 2018 (above).
- Letting misfortune float away (ปล่อยเคราะห์ / สะเดาะเคราะห์): stated by PR Dept (above) and by en.wikipedia "Yi Peng festival" (lead only, read 2026-10-03). A Lanna-studies primary text for this: [UNCONFIRMED].
- Older practice per a Chiang Mai culture network coordinator (เสาวคนธ์ ศรีบุญเรือง, เครือข่ายชุมชนเมืองรักษ์เชียงใหม่): ว่าวฮม was released by day at the temple, one per temple, once a year, after the morning chanting; mass night releases started about 20+ years earlier, around the 1995 SEA Games opening in Chiang Mai. [SECONDARY — her words as relayed from a Thai PBS "The North" video] — The News Lens, 22 Nov 2018; and Thai PBS, 14 Aug 2019 "โคมลอย... ประเพณี หรือ การท่องเที่ยว". https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/282884
- Chiang Mai's chamber-of-commerce vice-chair, also a 2025 festival organiser, puts sky-lantern release at Yi Peng at "about 20 years". [CONFIRMED as his statement] — Prachachat Turakij, 23 Nov 2025. https://www.prachachat.net/economic/local-economy/news-1924154
- Scholarly study of Lanna lantern-offering belief (worship / gratitude / faith principles, palm-leaf Dhamma texts, Mae Ka Phueak story). [CONFIRMED abstract] — Phra Natthakit Thitamethi, MCU Haripunchai Review 5(1), 2021. https://so04.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JMHR/article/view/252544
- Festival as tourism-reshaped tradition. [CONFIRMED abstract] — Arunothai Wanthaworn, "A Historical Study of Cultural Reproduction of the Yi Peng Tradition in Chiang Mai Province", 2021. https://so03.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/hssj/article/view/244939

## 4. Rules

### 4.1 Legal basis
- Air Navigation Act sections 59/1, 59/2 (as amended 2019, ฉบับที่ 14 พ.ศ. 2562): no launching lanterns etc. inside the air-navigation safety zone. Penalty up to 5 years and/or 200,000 baht; endangering an aircraft in service: death, life, or 15–20 years plus 600,000–800,000 baht fine. [CONFIRMED] — AOT press release 89/2567, 11 Nov 2024 (above).
- Provincial notices were issued under NCPO head's order 27/2559 (10 Jun 2016). [CONFIRMED] — Royal Gazette notices (above).
- Penalty if a lantern starts a fire: up to 7 years and/or 140,000 baht. [SECONDARY] — Thai PBS, 12 Nov 2024 https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/346239 ; MGR Online 18 Oct 2023 (above). Statute not read. [UNCONFIRMED which section]
- Red zone (พื้นที่เฝ้าระวังพิเศษระดับ 1) around a runway: 4.6 km either side of the runway, 18.5 km from each runway end (Samut Sakhon notice; Phuket uses 15 km). [CONFIRMED] — Royal Gazette notices (above).
- Max 100 lanterns per site per day; no fireworks tied to lanterns; at least 1 km from fuel stations; permit from the district chief (นายอำเภอ). [CONFIRMED for Samut Sakhon] — Royal Gazette 14 Nov 2016. Chiang Mai: permit from the district chief at least 30 days ahead; lanterns marked with symbols/colours for tracing. [SECONDARY] — Thai PBS 12 Nov 2024; Thai PBS 1 Nov 2022 https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/321056
